How it works

A private route through Tor, built for each place

Everything runs on your computer, inside the connector. When you ask for a place, it finds Tor exit relays there and builds a route that may leave the Tor network only through them.

Claudeyour chat, with the connector
Connectoron your computer, with its own isolated browser
Guardfirst Tor relay, first layer of encryption
Middlesecond relay, in another country
Exitthird relay, in the place you asked for
one Tor route and one browser for each active place, up to three at a time

Why changing place takes a moment

A new place means a new route. The connector reads the public list of Tor exit relays, keeps those in the place you asked for, and builds a circuit through three relays in different countries, agreeing a separate encryption key with each one, one after the other. That handshake is what keeps the route private. It takes a few seconds; then the place stays ready until you turn it off.

The place is verified, not assumed

After the route is built, the exit IP is geolocated through the tunnel with two independent services. If the exit is somewhere else, the connector tries another group of exits, up to three times. Otherwise Claude tells you why it could not.

Why Tor

Free, private by design, already inside the connector

Free

No subscription, no account, nothing to pay. Tor is run by the non-profit Tor Project and by volunteers.

Nothing else to install

Tor comes inside the connector, downloaded once from the Tor Project and checked against its published checksum.

Three layers of encryption

Each relay removes only its own layer: no single relay knows both who you are and where you are going.

No provider in the middle

No company sees all your traffic or keeps it, because there is no single provider.

A worldwide network

Thousands of volunteer relays, with exits in dozens of countries and many US states.

Names stay in the tunnel

Website names are resolved by Tor, not by your own connection.

Safety

What the connector already protects

  • Runs on your computerNo server to expose and no port open to the internet.
  • One route per placeEach place has its own Tor client and its own isolated browser.
  • Only public HTTP and HTTPSOther schemes, localhost and private, loopback and reserved addresses are refused.
  • No leaks on turn offThe browser is closed before its Tor client.
  • Untrusted pagesPages can contain prompt injection. Only address, title and status come back to Claude.
  • Checked downloadThe Tor Expert Bundle is compared with the SHA-256 sums published by the Tor Project before use.
Setup

Install once, then just write

No VPN, no account, no server, nothing to pay. You need Python and Claude Desktop.

Install the connector

pip install -r requirements.txt

Add it to Claude Desktop

Settings, Developer, Edit config, then add the connector with the path of server.py on your computer, and restart Claude.

"claude-vpn": { "command": "python", "args": ["C:/path/to/claude-vpn-mcp/server.py"] }

Write

"Activate my location in California." The first time, Tor and the browser engine download by themselves.

Limits

Good to know

Where Tor has exits

Many countries and US states have Tor exits; a few places have none at a given moment.

Some sites check Tor visitors

A few websites ask Tor visitors for an extra check, or block them.

Title and address only

browser_open does not return page contents or screenshots.

Geolocation is a database

The place check is as good as the geolocation services behind it.

Up to three places

One route and one browser per place, three places at a time.

Names and redirects

Private destinations are blocked by IP address; host names that resolve to private addresses are left to Tor's own exit rules.

Do not sign in to personal accounts or enter sensitive credentials in this browser.

FAQ

Questions

Do I need to install a VPN?

No. The connector uses Tor, which comes inside it. No VPN app, no account, no subscription.

Does it cost anything?

No. Tor is free and run by the non-profit Tor Project and volunteers, and the connector is free.

Why does changing place take a few seconds?

Because it builds a new private route through three Tor relays in different countries, agreeing a separate key with each one. Then the place stays ready until you turn it off.

Which traffic goes through Tor?

Only the traffic of the browser opened for that place. Everything else, Claude included, keeps its usual connection.

Which places can I ask for?

Any place with Tor exits: many countries and US states, and some cities. Every place is verified by geolocating the exit.

Is this made by Anthropic or the Tor Project?

No. Claude VPN MCP is an independent project by Monili VLR. It uses Tor and connects to Claude as an MCP connector.
